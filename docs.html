How Buildbond works

What it is

A launchpad on Robinhood Chain where each coin pays for its own app. The launcher writes one paragraph about the app and launches the coin on Pons. The coin's creator fees go to a vault made for it. When the vault's budget is large enough, an AI agent builds the app from the paragraph, checks it in a real browser, and publishes it. Every step it takes is logged on the coin's page, and every cent of model usage is billed to the vault with a receipt anyone can check.

A quarter of every coin's fees buys $BOND and burns it.

The split

The split happens inside harvest(), which anyone can call. It is fixed in the contract and cannot be changed by anyone.

60% budgetstays in the vault; leaves only through bill()
25% burnsent to the factory at once; the factory burns $BOND with it
15% launcherowed to the launcher; claimShare() pays it, anyone may call it
Fees paid in the coinburned at the dead address during the harvest
Top-upsany ETH sent to the vault goes straight into the budget

The agent

The agent is Gemini 2.5 Pro with six tools and nothing else: list, read, write and delete files in the app's folder, run the check, and finish. It cannot run shell commands, cannot reach the keys of the machine it runs on, and cannot publish anything that has not passed the check. It runs on a GitHub Actions machine, one job at a time.

The check opens the app in headless Chrome at 1280×800 and at 375×812 and fails on any uncaught error, console error, broken request, an almost empty page, or a page wider than a phone. A run ships only when the last check passes. A run stops when it has spent $3 of model usage or 80% of the budget, whichever is smaller.

Apps are static: HTML, CSS and JavaScript, published to GitHub Pages on their own origin. They get a wallet button, holder checks and a small shared store through the SDK below, so boards, guestbooks and trackers can be shared between people.

Bills and receipts

At the end of every run the site writes a receipt: the vault, the run, the version, the model, its list price, the input and output tokens, the ETH price from Chainlink, the amount in wei, the sha256 of the whole run log and the commit. The receipt's own sha256 is the hash in the bill() transaction. The coin page fetches the receipt and recomputes that hash in your browser.

Largest single bill0.02 ETH
Daily cap per vault0.05 ETH

$BOND burns

The factory keeps the 25% shares. When its pool is worth $5, the keeper calls burn(): the factory buys $BOND on its Pons curve, or in its Uniswap v4 pool after graduation, with the dead address as the recipient, and at least 97% of the simulated amount. The burn carries an attestation, the sha256 of the list of harvest transactions whose shares it spent, and the site lists those transactions with it. Nothing is paid to anyone.

Keeper rules

These live off chain, in the site's code, and decide when work is worth starting. The contract limits what a bill can take whatever they say.

Harvestwhen 0.0002 ETH or more of a coin's fees is waiting
First buildbudget worth $10 or more
New version12 hours after the last run, same budget rule
Burnfactory pool worth $5 or more
Failed runsafter 3 in a row, a coin rests for a day
Scheduleevery 10 minutes, one build at a time

Risks

Not audited. The agent can build something that works badly, and a coin's app is only as good as the brief and the budget behind it. The builder is a hot wallet run by the platform; the contract caps what it can bill, and changing it takes 48 hours in public, but it can bill up to those caps. Coins can go to zero. Apps depend on GitHub Pages, the shared store and the model provider staying available.
